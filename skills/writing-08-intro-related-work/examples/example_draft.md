## Research Background

Transitioning optical sensors from controlled laboratories into outdoor monitoring introduces rapid thermal and humidity swings that degrade baseline stability [@chen2024].

## Related Work

Under outdoor calibration stability targets, static thermal lookup models [@chen2024] hold indoor error near 2.8% but deteriorate above 75% RH, whereas active Peltier stabilization [@patel2023] achieves 1.2% error across 15-40 C at the cost of 6.5 W continuous power draw.

## Research Gaps

Together, static lookup calibration [@chen2024] and powered Peltier loops [@patel2023] leave unresolved how to limit calibration drift under outdoor humidity and thermal transitions while staying within sub-watt battery budgets.

## Contributions

We evaluate an unpowered passive-reference calibration scheme in a 30-run outdoor protocol (Field protocol, Table 1), reducing mean calibration drift from 3.4% to 1.3% below 0.2 W across 15-40 C and 45-85% RH. Because measurements come from one coastal site, arid-climate transfer remains unverified.
